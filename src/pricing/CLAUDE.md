# atelier-api — pricing

Extracted from the root CLAUDE.md (root conventions still apply). Loaded automatically when working in this directory.

## The waterfall and its inputs

- **`waterfall.js` is the ONE engine, and it is pure.** `computeWaterfall(input)` takes ten inputs and returns every step, `landed`, `trade_sell`, `retail_sell` and `missing`. Order is the verified incumbent order — duty BEFORE freight, clearance over both — and it never substitutes zero: a null input names itself in `missing` and every step downstream is null. The vendored demo waterfall in `src/seed/data/pricing.js` has the wrong order and invented constants and is NOT imported anywhere; the CRM's Pricing screen ran on it until September 2026, which is when the client asked whether the numbers were made up. They were.
- **Since September 2026 pricing runs on the platform's OWN parameters, and Dynamics is a reference.** The brief: "the pricing waterfall shouldn't be a Dynamics tracking option, it should be based on our existing waterfall logic, customisable per brand/supplier." `pricing-parameters.js` is that: the parameter rows, the exchange rates and the resolver that turns a house into a full input set, each input carrying its SOURCE (`INPUT_SOURCES`: `house`, `supplier`, `terms`, `freight-ledger`, `fx`, `none`, `missing`). `/admin/freight/landed-preview` is the older, Dynamics-driven preview and stays for the recovery work; `/admin/pricing/waterfall` is the one the Pricing screen uses.
- **Where each input lives — and there is exactly one place for each.** Discount and retail markup: `supplier_brand_terms` (flat / multiplicative chain / tiers, resolved by `src/suppliers/discount-chain.js` to ONE net percentage — {0.4, 0.4} is 64%, not 80%). Freight: the dated `supplier_freight_rates` ledger — the HOUSE's own rate first (a range-name scope under the house's name; the client sets freight per house, Sept 2026), then the supplier-wide rate. A range name that no house carries is refused by the form and the rate sheet (`unknownHouses`), so an imported rate cannot silently never match. Exchange rate: `fx_rates` by the SUPPLIER's invoicing currency (the house's own currency only where no supplier is linked). Everything else — duty bands A/B/C, clearance, trade markup, FX margin, handling, and the small-order surcharge (`surcharge_min_qty`, `surcharge_per_unit`, migration 1791840000000 — house parameters the waterfall never reads) — is `supplier_pricing_parameters`. Do not add a second home for any of them: the resolver's precedence is the only rule, and a second column is a second rule.
- **`supplier_pricing_parameters` is one row per SCOPE, house override over supplier default, every column nullable.** `(supplier_id IS NULL) <> (brand_id IS NULL)` (the freight ledger's scope CHECK) plus a partial unique index per scope. A house row states only the fields it overrides; the resolver reads house, then the house's supplier, then `missing`. **The write is a PUT, not a PATCH**: the body is the whole set for that scope and an omitted field is CLEARED (`upsertScope` writes every column). A patch would let a stale duty band survive beside a fresh clearance with no way to tell which the office last looked at.
- **A blank duty CLASS is 0% duty and is source `none`, not `missing`.** It is a fact about the price line (no duty letter, the rule the D365 corpus proved on all 17,749 such rows), not an unset parameter. A duty class whose band is unset IS missing. Percentages are stored as the office writes them (5 is 5%); `retail_markup` is a multiplier (1.30) because that is the only one the corpus states as a ratio.
- **What-if overrides are a preview-only input, source `manual`.** `GET /admin/pricing/waterfall` takes any of `OVERRIDABLE_INPUTS` as a query parameter and `applyOverrides` stands it in for the resolved input, recording what it replaced in `detail.replaced`; nothing is written. This is the Pricing screen's Manual mode — type a number, watch the waterfall move, then Save, which is a separate PUT. A blank or negative what-if is ignored, never applied as zero; a freight what-if is dollars per unit (a percentage basis can only come from the ledger).
- **Every `/admin/pricing/*` route is finance-gated, reads included**, through `requireFinance` from `src/reports/access.js` — the one gate, recomputed per request. There is no masked view of a parameter editor. Body schemas are structural (types, non-negativity); `PARAMETER_COLUMNS` is the real gate on what reaches SQL, because Fastify's Ajv strips unknown keys rather than rejecting them.
- **Supplier discounts price PURCHASE ORDERS only; a sale is priced at the house's own discount (1 Oct 2026, migration 1792963200000).** The office ruled it after Armani Casa sold at its supplier's 50% while Dynamics prices the range at 0%: the house page's discount and the supplier's terms were one row. `supplier_pricing_parameters.discount_percent` is the house's own figure (a percentage, a house row only — a CHECK refuses it on a supplier row). It sits OUTSIDE `PARAMETER_COLUMNS`, so a PUT of the set never clears it; `getHouseDiscount` / `setHouseDiscount` read and write it. `resolveWaterfallInputs`/`previewWaterfall` take `purchase`: without it (trade, retail, the product-prices page, the sync, the waterfall route) the discount is the house's own or `missing` — never the terms, never `suppliers.default_discount`; with it (`po-pricing.js` only) it is the supplier's terms at the line's discount class, then the supplier's averaged rate. Discount CLASSES are the supplier's terms, so they price purchase orders alone and a product's or variation's discount letter moves no sell price. The migration carried over each flat terms rate whose note says the ranges import or the house page wrote it; a rate from a supplier agreement stayed with the supplier, and its house reads `missing` until the next ranges upload (blank = 0%).
- **`parameters-backfill.js` puts the Dynamics tracking-options export onto the houses** — every figure in its one home: duty bands, clearance, trade markup, surcharge and the discount on the house's parameter set; freight as a dated row on the ledger under the house's name. Two callers, one planner: `npm run backfill:pricing-parameters [-- --dry-run] [-- --overwrite]` reads the staging mirror, and the CRM's upload (below) hands in the rows it read out of the workbook (`rangeParameters` is injectable — also what keeps `test/pricing-parameters.test.js` and `test/house-pricing.test.js` off the real houses). Rules:
  - **Fill only what is blank, unless `overwrite`.** A NULL column fills; a house with no freight rate of its own gets one. Every saved value the export disagrees with is a `kept` change line — the dry run says exactly what a replace would do — and `overwrite` turns those into `replace` (a parameter column set, freight as a NEW dated row; the old rate stays as history).
  - **Never touched either way:** the supplier's terms (they price purchase orders), and anything the export leaves BLANK (a blank duty band is no defined duty, blank freight is unknown; neither clears a saved value). Inactive ranges are skipped.
  - **A blank Dynamics discount is a stated 0%** (verified on 4,638 rows).
  - **Freight is a figure, with no unit** (1 Oct 2026, the office: "those values will be separate from any kind of unit"). The unit is the PRODUCT's (`products.unit`, below); a house's first rate is written whatever price lines it has (it used to need a unit from them, and Alpaka, Armani Casa, Missoni Home and S. Harris got no freight for want of one). A replacement is a new dated row. A house whose rate is already dated today cannot take a second one (`freightDatedToday`) — the ledger's one-rate-per-day index. F1 is answered (freight is per house), which is why the export's figure may now be written at all.
  - Freight is written through `createFreightRate` — never a direct INSERT. The terms tables may not even be NAMED outside `src/suppliers/` (`test/suppliers-terms.test.js`, a substring scan that catches comments too).
  - FX and handling are not in the export.
- **One house's pricing is `house-pricing.js`, served as `GET|PUT /admin/pricing/houses/:id`** — the house page's Pricing panel in the CRM. The read is the house's own parameter set beside its supplier's default set, its discount (`percent` — the house's own, `source` house/missing, always `editable` — and `classes`, the supplier's), its own every-class freight rate beside the supplier's, `freight_classes` (its rate per letter), `freight_class_lines` (the letters its price lines carry, with counts). The write is ONE transaction over the three homes: `parameters` is the whole set (PUT — an omitted column clears); `discount_percent` optional (the house's own: absent = leave it, null clears it, a number sets it; no supplier needed); `discount_classes` optional, the WHOLE set of classes (below); `freight` optional `{ rate, effective_from? }` appended only when it differs from the current rate; `freight_classes` optional, a rate per letter appended the same way. Classes 422 on a chain house or a house with no supplier. Percentages throughout, the discount and its classes included — the terms' fraction is converted here. An archived house reads and writes too (the page shows it).
- **Discount, freight and duty class letters live on the product and the variation (1 Oct 2026, migrations 1792531200000 and 1792617600000).** `duty_class` (A–C, the house's duty bands) sits beside the other two: the product's is filled from its Dynamics price's inferred duty class (NULL = no duty, as when Dynamics' two duty prices agree), a variation's NULL is its product's, and it re-prices like the others (`dutyClass` through the resolver, pricer, sync and reprice). `products.discount_class` / `freight_class` and `colourways.discount_class` / `freight_class`, A–E; a variation's null is its product's, a product's null is the house's default class and every-class freight. `resolveWaterfallInputs` takes `discountClass` / `freightClass` and, given `productId` (+ `colourwayId`), the letters chosen there (`productClasses`: the variation's own, else the product's; 422 for another house's product or another product's variation). The product-price pricer takes `price(row, { freightClass, dutyClass })` — the product's freight letter over the row's Dynamics one. **Who is priced where:** the upload sync prices each product at its own letters and fills a blank `freight_class` from the row's Dynamics letter (`freight_class_filled`; 20,449 of 20,478 products on a cutover copy); a product PATCH that moves the wholesale rate or a freight or duty letter re-prices the product (`repriceProduct`) — the discount letter does not, it prices purchase orders only (above) — and every variation with a letter of its own (`repriceOwnClassVariations`); a variation PATCH that sets a letter prices the variation at it, and clearing both sends it back to NULL (its product's price) — a price typed in the same save wins. A hand-priced row is not re-priced. Purchase-order lines price at the line's variation's letters (`po-pricing.js`). Dynamics states no discount class per product (one discount per range on all 19,327 archived prices), so only the freight letter is synced. `GET /admin/pricing/waterfall` takes `discount_class` and `colourway_id`.
- **Units live on the product, not on freight (1 Oct 2026, migration 1792444800000).** `supplier_freight_rates.unit` is gone with its CHECKs: a rate is a figure (dollars, or a percentage under `basis`), and what it is per is the product's `products.unit` (metre, yard, roll, piece, hide, panel; NULL not stated), edited on the product (`PATCH /admin/products/:id { unit }`). The migration filled it from the product's own Dynamics pack type (the archived product price it was built from), then its house's freight unit. The freight form, the house page, the ranges import and the waterfall no longer take or return a unit; the yearly rate sheet dropped its Unit column and still READS a sheet that has one (`FREIGHT_RATE_SHEET_HEADER_V2`), ignoring it. `houseUnits` is deleted. Not the colourway's stock unit (`inventory.unit`, from `product_type`).
- **Freight classes (Sept 2026, migration 1792012800000): `supplier_freight_rates.freight_class`, one letter, NULL = every class.** Every price line carries a freight letter (`price_list_items.freight_class`, A–E) and the incumbent priced freight per (range × letter) — Fabricut A $30 against B $3. `resolveFreightRate({ …, freight_class })` prefers the letter's rate, then the every-class rate; the class OUTRANKS the category (the letter is how the line ships). A caller naming no class gets only every-class rates, never a letter's — which is what keeps the import's `currentRangeRates` and every existing caller unchanged. The unique index gained `COALESCE(freight_class, '')`: a default and an A rate may share a day, two A rates may not. `previewWaterfall`/`resolveWaterfallInputs` take `freightClass` (the waterfall route's `freight_class`), and `inputs.freight.detail.freight_class` says which rate answered. The yearly sheet carries a `Freight Class` column after Category and still READS the pre-class header (`FREIGHT_RATE_SHEET_HEADER_V1`) — its rows are every-class rates. Class rates are append-only like any other: the house page adds and changes them, never removes one.
- **A freight class can be REMOVED** (`remove_freight_classes: ['B']` on `PUT /admin/pricing/houses/:id`, 28 Sep 2026 — the office: classes could be added but not removed). `deleteHouseClassRates` deletes every row of that letter under the house (dated ahead included), so its lines pay the house's every-class rate again; the one exception to the ledger's append-only rule, because an end-dated row would be a rate of nothing. A letter both set and removed in one save is a 422. The house's own rate and other letters are untouched.
- **Discount classes are the house's supplier-terms TIERS, edited from the house page** (no schema change). They are the supplier's discount and price purchase orders only. `discount_classes: [{ id?, label?, discount_class?, percent, is_default? }]` is the whole set: an `id` keeps/edits that tier, no `id` creates one, a tier left out is DELETED (`deleteSupplierDiscountTier`, scoped in the statement). Each class carries its LETTER (A–E, one per house, 1 Oct 2026); a class with only a letter is named after it. A product or variation that chose a removed class's letter keeps the letter and falls back to category matching and the default, which is why the read carries each class's `products` count (products plus variations with their own letter, `countByDiscountClass`). A letter moving between classes is cleared before it is set. Written in the order the two tier indexes need: removals, then the old default cleared, then edits, then new tiers. A house with no terms gets a terms row (no flat discount) to hang them on. On a purchase order a flat terms rate covers what no class does (resolveTermsChain: chain > chosen tier > category label > default tier > flat).
- **The upload is `POST /admin/pricing/houses/import/validate|apply?overwrite=&filename=`**, the workbook's raw bytes (xlsx / octet-stream parser registered inside the pricing plugin), finance-gated like every `/admin/pricing/*` route. `house-pricing-import.js` checks the entity FIRST (`d365WorkbookEntity` — another export would otherwise fail on a list of ninety missing columns), reads the rest through `scripts/lib/d365-workbook.js` (lifted out of `convert-d365-exports.js` unchanged; the converter's output was byte-identical before and after) and `toRangeParameters` (the staging mapper, exported), so an uploaded file and a staged one are one reading. A per-cell problem is a 422 carrying `errors` (the route replies with the body itself — Fastify's default serialiser drops extra fields). Validate plans inside a transaction and rolls back; apply re-reads the FILE and re-plans against the live state. Neither writes to `legacy_tracking_options`.
- **`tracking-parameters.js`, `freight-recovery.js` are READ-ONLY views of the Dynamics staging mirror** (`legacy_tracking_options`) — the parameters the incumbent priced with, served by `/admin/freight/parameters` and shown in the CRM as a reference beside the office's own values. `freight` there is dollars per unit, `clearance` a percentage, blank discount 0, blank freight UNKNOWN (never zero); Type rows carry no parameter at all. Nothing in this directory writes to a `legacy_*` table.

## Product prices — the Dynamics product-prices reimport (25 Sep 2026)

- **What it is.** The client's "Active Product Prices" export (21,102 rows, 16 columns) landed in `product_prices` (migration `1791926400000`) and priced, per row, through the house its range names. `product-prices-import.js` reads and applies the upload; `product-prices.js` is the pricer and the reads; `src/routes/admin-product-prices.js` serves `/admin/product-prices*` (finance-gated, reads included; its own plugin because it registers the workbook parser). Guarded by `test/product-prices.test.js`.
- **The archive is never written.** `legacy_product_prices` holds the FULL export (45 columns) and `importLegacyEntity` upserts every mapped column to EXCLUDED, so the narrow view landed there would null out the rest. The client said explicitly: do not overwrite or remove the archive. A test asserts the archive's row is byte-identical after an apply. The table is not `legacy_`-prefixed (three suites count that namespace).
- **Its own reader, same rules.** `readD365Workbook` refuses any file missing one of the archive's columns — i.e. every file of this view — so `readProductPricesWorkbook` reads the same envelope by LOGICAL name with its own column map and a five-column required set. A bad row refuses the whole file; a range with no house is NOT a bad row (it lands and prices once the house exists).
- **The price is derived on read, never stored.** The client's formula (ELLA STRIPE, MADEAUX: 47.60 ÷ 0.38 = 125.26; + freight 50; × 1.10 = 192.79; × 1.87 = 360.52) is `computeWaterfall` step for step, plus the range's discount before the exchange (the export proves it on 20,651 of 20,977 rows). The pricer resolves each house ONCE per category through `resolveWaterfallInputs` and prices every row from that; editing a house reprices its range at once.
- **Four deliberate differences from the house waterfall, each the client's formula:** (1) the exchange rate is the ROW's currency's `fx_rates` row, not the supplier's; NZD is 1. (2) FX margin and handling are not steps of the range formula — an UNSET one is `0`, source `none`, saying so; a value finance HAS set is applied. (3) Duty: `duty_class` is inferred from Dynamics' two prices (incl > ex ⇒ 'A', the only letter the corpus ever used) and resolved to the house's band; two prices that agree are 0% (the ELLA STRIPE example). A row Dynamics priced BY HAND ("Set prices manually" — both prices blank: Alpaka, Garrett Leather, Loro Piana, Brochier, C & C Milano, Atelier Stock … 54 rows on 28 Sep 2026) is NOT run through the range formula at all (1 Oct 2026): its trade sell is Dynamics' own (`hand_priced: true`, `steps: null`, difference 0) — Dynamics built it from a duty % (5), a markup and a freight typed on the product, freight after the markup and no clearance, often rounded by hand, none of which an export carries — and its retail is that × (1 + the house's retail markup). Until then these rows had no trade and so no retail, whatever markup the house carried (Alpaka). A hand-priced product's wholesale edit does not re-price it (`product-reprice.js` says so). (4) Freight is the HOUSE's (F1) **at the row's letter**: the house's freight-class rate for `freight_letter` where it has one, its every-class rate otherwise (30 Sep 2026 — the letter was never passed, so a class rate set on the house page never reached this page).
- **Where Dynamics disagrees, and why (measured 30 Sep 2026 on a cutover copy with the 28 Sep exports).** Of 12,299 rows that price, 4,417 agreed and 7,876 differed — and every one of the 7,876 parts AFTER duty: exchange rate, discount and duty all agree. It is freight. Dynamics charges a rate per (range × letter) (Fabricut A $30 / B $3 / C $5, Pierre Frey A $30 / B $35 / C $10, Jean Monro B $30 against $50), while a house carries the ranges export's ONE figure (Fabricut 23), and several ranges' letter A has moved since Dynamics last saved its prices (Fabricut, Trend, Stroheim, Clarence House, Vervain: $30 in the prices, $20–23 on the range). With the letter passed and those 34 per-letter rates set as freight classes, 12,257 of 12,299 agree to the cent; the last 42 are prices typed as round figures in Dynamics (hangers $165, pillows $750, samples $90) and three hand-priced C & C Milano rows. Hand-priced rows follow a different Dynamics formula — wholesale ÷ rate × (1 + Duty %) × (1 + Markup to Trade %) + a manual freight, no discount, no clearance, freight after the markup (Alpaka 130 EUR: 288.89 × 1.05 × 1.80 + 15 = 561.00) — which this pricer does not reproduce; the manual freight is not in the prices export. Every row carries `difference`/`agrees`; the CRM shows both numbers and says why.
- **The house match is the ranges import's rule, copied exactly** (`brandFor` in `parameters-backfill.js`): name trimmed + case-insensitive with the NEWEST live house winning a tie, then the range's slug. Two rules would split a product from the house its range figures landed on. The `brand_id` list filter uses the pricer's rule too, not SQL name equality.
- **Exchange rates come from the export's Exchange Rate column** (`exchangerate`, added to the view by 28 Sep 2026: EUR 0.45, USD 0.51, GBP 0.38; read, never stored on `product_prices`). The client tied the rate to this import, so a STATED rate (≥ 90% of the currency's rows agree) is filled where `fx_rates` has none (`fill`, re-checked inside the lock) and REPLACES one that differs (`update`) — each `fx` line carries `source: 'file'|'vote'`, the apply answers `fx_filled` and `fx_updated`, and the note names the file. The house and product pages show and edit the same row (CRM `CurrencyFields`), so a rate typed there lasts until the next export that states another. An older export without the column falls back to the VOTE (price ex duty = wholesale × (1 − discount) ÷ rate, over rows whose house discount resolved): a voted rate only fills, never replaces — a disagreement is `differs`; with no house discounts yet the vote is `unclear`.
- **Re-import semantics.** Upsert on the D365 GUID; `last_import_id` marks what the latest export carried; a row a later export drops is kept and flagged (`in_latest_export: false`) and hidden from the list unless `include_previous`. Re-applying the same file is 0 new / 0 changed. Validate and apply are two routes; apply re-reads the file under an advisory lock.
- **Paging is `page` (1-based), as the CRM's list kit sends** — an `offset` param would be stripped by Ajv and every page would read as page 1.
- **Local testing without Postgres**: `@electric-sql/pglite-socket` (`pglite-server --db=memory:// --port=55432 --max-connections=20`, installed outside the repo) runs every migration and this suite; suites that race two real transactions hang on it, so it is not a substitute for CI.
- **Two markups and a clearance basis (28 Sep 2026, migration `1792358400000`).**
  - **Retail = trade × (1 + the house's `retail_markup_percent`)** — Dynamics' rule (retail ÷ trade is 1.30 on 17,692 of 19,271 archive rows; 1.35–1.80 on a dozen ranges). A house parameter like trade markup (house over supplier, PUT semantics — a caller that omits it CLEARS it). The older `retail_markup` multiplier on LANDED (supplier terms) is read only where no percentage is set. The product-price sync writes `retail_price_cents` fill-only beside trade.
  - **The ranges import fills it, though the ranges export has no such column** (the D365 tracking option has only a trade markup): `readSources` counts Dynamics' own retail ÷ trade — by guid over the latest product-prices import joined to `legacy_product_prices` (so a range renamed on the way in counts under its new name), and the archive's rows by range name for a range none of whose rows match (Dynamics re-created Stroheim, S. Harris, Clarence House and Vervain since the archive). A house takes the ratio most of its rows share as a percentage (1.30 → 30), fill-only like every other figure; no majority, or fewer than `MIN_RETAIL_ROWS` (5), is reported in `retailMarkupUnclear` (Jean Monro: one row).
  - **A product price's own markup to trade** (`product_prices.trade_markup_percent`, Dynamics' "Markup to Trade %", 39 of 21,054 rows) outranks the house's in the pricer, source `product` with the house's figure in `detail.replaced`. It arrives on the Product prices upload: a file carrying `atelier_manualmarkuptotrade` and no `atelier_wholesalerate` is a MARKUP export (`isMarkupExport`) — read by `readProductMarkupsWorkbook`, matched on the guid (an unknown one is counted, never created), a blank clearing it back to the range's — and a prices export that carries the column writes markups too. It is never in the upsert's `STORED_FIELDS`, so an export WITHOUT the column never clears one. The rows whose markup moved are re-priced with `overwrite` under source `import` (`productPriceIds` on the sync), because the price they carry was the import's own from the range's markup. A markup file logs NO `product_price_imports` row (that would move "the latest import" everywhere).
  - **The ranges import also fills each house's freight CLASSES from Dynamics' own prices** (30 Sep 2026, `letterFreights` in `parameters-backfill.js`). Dynamics charged freight per range × letter from a table no export carries — and not the range's own freight figure (Fabricut's range says $23; every lettered Fabricut price used $30 / $3 / $5; Pierre Frey A is $29.80, Jim Thompson A $29.50). The archive keeps each price's steps, so a letter's rate solves back exactly: `landed ÷ (1 + the range's Dynamics clearance) − price incl duty`, rounded to 10c, hand-priced rows excluded, latest-import rows under their (renamed) range, other archive rows under their own. `FREIGHT_CLASS_SHARE` (90%) of at least `MIN_FREIGHT_CLASS_ROWS` (5) must agree; otherwise `freightClassUnclear` (typed prices). A letter the house's every-class rate already charges gets no class; a class the house has is `kept` or, with overwrite, re-dated; change lines are `freight_class_<letter>`, written through `createFreightRate`. Measured on a cutover copy with the 28 Sep exports: 37 classes on 18 houses, after which every priced row of Fabricut, Trend, Pierre Frey, Stroheim, Clarence House, Vervain, Madeaux, Fadini Borghi and Fox Linton agrees with Dynamics to the cent (Jim Thompson: all but 12 typed prices). Discount, exchange rate, duty, clearance and trade markup already agreed on every archived row of those houses.
- **`clearance_basis`** ('percent', NULL reading the same, or 'dollar') says how to read `clearance_percent` — the column keeps its name. Dollar: `landed_ex_handling = price incl duty + freight + clearance`; percent: `× (1 + clearance/100)`. The basis comes from the SAME row as the value (`clearanceInput`), so a house's dollars are never read by its supplier's basis; a what-if clearance keeps the house's basis. The ranges import writes the figure only; the basis is the office's.
- **A price the cutover merge copied from Dynamics counts as blank** (28 Sep 2026). The deploy runs `merge:legacy`, whose `landStock` filled a blank trade/retail from D365's stored figure by SKU — so price-catalogue products whose colour SKU the D365 catalogue shared got Dynamics' retail (Nura $238.51 against $257.47 calculated). `landStock` now skips any product with a `product_price_id`, and `planProductSync` treats a price whose latest move was `source 'import', source_file 'merge:legacy'` as blank, so the next product-prices upload replaces it. A price a person or an upload set is still kept.
- **Editing a product's wholesale rate re-prices it** (`product-reprice.js`, called by `updateProduct`, 28 Sep 2026 — the office: "when we change the wholesale rate, the trade price remains the same"). A patch whose `wholesale_rate` differs from the saved one prices the product's own product-price row (its house, duty class, freight letter and own markup) with the NEW rate in the product's currency, and writes trade and retail where the formula yields them. A price typed in the same patch wins; the recalculated one is recorded as `manual` under the person's reason, else "Recalculated from the wholesale rate (a → b CUR)". A product with no price row, or a house that cannot price it, keeps its prices. The PATCH answer carries `product.repricing` (`{ repriced, trade_price_cents?, retail_price_cents?, reason? }`) only when the rate moved; the CRM's save message reads it. The product-price upload stays fill-only afterwards, so the edit survives a re-upload (reported as `kept`).
- **Both pages follow the link, not the name (1 Oct 2026).** The product price page's "In the catalogue" (`getProductPrice`) lists the products built from the row (`product_price_id`) plus an UNLINKED product carrying its description or code under the house, archived ones excluded; the product page's "Price from its range" asks `GET /admin/product-prices?product_id=` — the linked row (any import), else house + name. Both matched by name alone until then, and a row's own product is named "SETA FIAMMATA 4741 (1)": on a cutover copy 7,270 of 20,478 product pages found no row and 12,258 price pages listed an archived D365 product instead (Armani Casa 4741). The same rule as `planProductSync`.
- **Every row is a product (28 Sep 2026).** The apply creates a product for each row the catalogue lacks and links it (`products.product_price_id`); the sync then prices that product from its own row only. See the catalogue guide's "The catalogue from product prices".
- **Retail also comes from the trade price a product already carries** (1 Oct 2026, `planProductSync`): where the range cannot reach a trade sell (a house missing freight or a discount) but the product has a trade price above zero and its house a retail markup, retail = that trade × (1 + the markup). Fill-only like every other figure; a trade of 0 (the merge's placeholder) never makes a retail.
- **The range price is written onto the catalogue product** (`product-price-sync.js`, 25 Sep 2026 — the office: the price showed on the product price but not "on the product itself in Catalogue → Products"). A design named by a price row (house by the pricer's `houseFor`, name = description or code) takes `trade_price_cents` (the trade sell, NZ$), `wholesale_rate` (only where `products.currency` IS the row's currency — never converted) and `category` (from Type, blank only, vocabulary-checked). **Fill-only on every upload** — the product-prices apply and the colours apply both end with it, source `import` + the file's name — **and on every pricing save** (30 Sep 2026: a house given a retail markup after the upload left its products with no retail price, because nothing re-ran the sync). Each `PUT` under `/admin/pricing/*` and the ranges import apply sync after they land and answer `catalogue`: the house page and `parameters/brands/:id` over that house's products (`brandIds`), a supplier default, an exchange rate and the import over every product, source `import` with a `source_file` naming the cause ("the house's range figures said so"); a price the product already carries and disagrees with is `kept` and reported. **Replace is a person's act**: `POST /admin/product-prices/:id/apply-to-product { product_id }` (the product page's "Set as the trade price"), source `manual` with the reason "Set to the price from its range (<code>, <range>)". A design two rows name at different prices is `ambiguous` and untouched; retail is never written (the formula stops at trade). Every write runs under `withChangeContext` — the capture trigger refuses an undeclared price move, and `product_price_history.product_id` is RESTRICT, so a test deleting products must delete their history first.
